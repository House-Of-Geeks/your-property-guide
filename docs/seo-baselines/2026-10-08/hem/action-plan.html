02Action 1

A1

A HEM guide that states the five facts in the first paragraph and carries an indicative table

pagefirst

Why

Every page the AI Overviews cite says the same five things in its first 150 words: Melbourne Institute; median spend on basics plus the 25th percentile on discretionary items; a floor under declared expenses; varies by household, income band and location; excludes housing. Ours says one of them. The People Also Ask list adds the questions nobody answers from the source documents: does HEM include rent, HEM or my actual spending, how far back do banks look, is HEM the same at every bank.

Build

  • URL: /guides/household-expenditure-measure-hem, with the glossary page kept as the short definition and linking down (no equity to redirect).
  • Order: one-paragraph definition; who makes it and from what; how it is built; what it includes and what it leaves out; the indicative table by household type and income band, dated, labelled "Your Property Guide estimate" with the method stated, because the licensed table is not public and every ranking page publishes an indicative one; how lenders apply it (APRA's greater-of rule quoted from APG 223, ASIC's "plausibility check, not a substitute" from RG 209, the Westpac outcome in two sentences); what banks verify on statements and for how long; what moves borrowing capacity and what does not; a worked example (couple, $180,000, Sydney, two children); FAQ with FAQPage schema.
  • FAQ from the PAA and forums: Does HEM include rent? · Do lenders use HEM or my actual expenses? · Is HEM the same at every bank? · Can I lower my expenses before I apply, and does it help? · What counts as a living expense on an application? · Is spending $3,000 a month a lot? · What does HEM stand for in finance? · How is HEM calculated?
  • Length: 2,500 to 3,000 words. Sources: official-facts.md. The Melbourne Institute page blocks scripted fetches; read it in a browser before quoting.

03Action 2

A2

A HEM and living-expenses calculator, embedded in the guide and at its own URL

toolwith A1

Why

"hem calculator" is 390 searches a month and autocomplete adds "australia", "nsw", "victoria", "nab", "based on salary", "based on income" and "hem for single person / couple / family of 3 / 4 / 5". Google's AI Overview for the query says there is no public HEM calculator, and the top result is a broker's page. Home Loan Experts and Bank Australia hold the "living expenses calculator" variant. Nothing in the results shows the reader the figure a lender will use for their household and what it does to their borrowing capacity.

Build

  • URL: /hem-calculator on CalculatorPageLayout with WebApplication schema, same pattern as the FHSS and bridging calculators.
  • Inputs: household (single, couple), dependants, gross household income band, state, capital or regional, then optional declared expenses by the categories lenders ask for (groceries, utilities, transport, insurance, childcare, education, subscriptions, entertainment, other).
  • Outputs: indicative HEM for that household, monthly and annual, dated and labelled; the reader's declared total beside it; which figure a lender would use and why; the borrowing-capacity difference, handed to the borrowing power calculator with the figures pre-filled.
  • Data: one indicative table in src/lib/data/hem.ts (household type by income band, with a regional adjustment), the same table A3 uses, with its method and date in a comment and on the page. Pure computation in src/lib/hem-calc.ts with tests.

04Action 3

A3

Scale the borrowing power calculator's HEM, show the figure used, and call the page a serviceability calculator

fixwith A1

Why

The calculator floors expenses at $2,000, $2,500, $3,000, $3,500 or $4,000 a month by number of dependants. APRA's guide says a prudent lender scales the index by income, and the real measure varies by household composition and location as well. The result is shown without saying which benchmark produced it. Separately, "serviceability calculator" and its lender variants are about 900 searches a month of commercial intent and the page never uses the word.

Build

  • Replace HEM_BASE in src/lib/utils/borrowing-power.ts with a lookup into the shared indicative table (household, dependants, income band, region); keep the greater-of logic.
  • In the results, print "Living expenses used: $X a month (your figure / the indicative HEM benchmark for your household, [date])" with a link to the guide.
  • Title and H1 become "Borrowing Power and Serviceability Calculator"; add an FAQ item "What is a serviceability calculator?" and one on the 3% buffer; update the glossary "serviceability" entry to link here.
