# Household Expenditure Measure: the rules and facts, checked 8 October 2026

Used to check our pages and to brief the guide. Sources are APRA's prudential practice guide, ASIC's regulatory guide and media releases, the Full Federal Court's judgment, the Melbourne Institute's description of the measure, and the lenders' own pages. Where a figure comes from a comparison site rather than the Melbourne Institute it is marked indicative, because the HEM tables themselves are licensed to lenders and not published.

## What the HEM is

- A benchmark of household living expenses produced by the **Melbourne Institute of Applied Economic and Social Research** (University of Melbourne), listed among its social indicator reports alongside the Henderson Poverty Lines [MI].
- Built from the ABS **Household Expenditure Survey**. The standard description, repeated by Finder, Savings.com.au, Infochoice and the lenders, is the **median** spend on "absolute basics" (food, utilities, transport, communications, children's clothing and the like) plus the **25th percentile** spend on "discretionary basics" (takeaway, alcohol, entertainment, adult clothing) [F][S][I].
- It varies by **household composition** (single, couple, number of dependants), **income band** and **location**, and is updated **quarterly** [F][S][APG223 para on indices "typically quarterly"].
- It **excludes housing costs** (rent or mortgage) and lenders treat items such as private school fees, childcare, insurances, HECS and debt repayments as separate commitments on top of HEM [I][F].
- The figures are **not public**. Lenders license them; the numbers on comparison sites are their own estimates. Indicative monthly figures published in 2026: single adult about $1,800 to $2,200; couple about $2,400 to $3,000; couple with two children about $2,900 to $3,400 (mid-income band, JMD Mortgages, 2026); Infochoice's example family about $3,100; propertyed's range $1,800 to $3,800 [J][I][P]. Treat all of these as indicative.

## How lenders are expected to use it

- **APRA, APG 223 Residential Mortgage Lending (July 2019)**: "ADIs typically use the Household Expenditure Measure (HEM) or the Henderson Poverty Index (HPI) in loan calculators to estimate a borrower's living expenses. Although these indices are extensively used, they might not always be an appropriate proxy of a borrower's actual living expenses. Reliance solely on these indices generally would therefore not meet APRA's requirements for sound risk management. APRA therefore expects ADIs to use the greater of a borrower's declared living expenses or an appropriately scaled version of the HEM or HPI indices. That is, if the HEM or HPI is used, a prudent ADI would apply a margin linked to the borrower's income to the relevant index. In addition, an ADI would update these indices in loan calculators on a frequent basis, or at least in line with published updates of these indices (typically quarterly)." [APG223]
- **ASIC, RG 209 Credit licensing: Responsible lending conduct (9 December 2019)**: a benchmark "does not provide any positive confirmation of what the particular consumer's income and expenses actually are"; the HEM "reflects 'low-budget' spending and it is expected that many consumers would in fact have higher living expenses"; benchmarks are "a useful tool to help determine whether information provided about general living expenses is plausible", but using a benchmark in place of declared expenses is "a substitute for reasonable inquiries and steps to verify", not a form of them; ASIC also clarified that a benchmark floor is not required [RG209][REP643].
- **ASIC v Westpac** ("wagyu and shiraz"): Perram J found for Westpac in 2019; the Full Federal Court dismissed ASIC's appeal 2:1 on 26 June 2020 ([2020] FCAFC 111) and ordered ASIC to pay costs; ASIC announced on 20 July 2020 it would not seek special leave. The practical result is that using HEM in an automated serviceability assessment is lawful; APRA's prudential expectation (greater of declared and scaled HEM) is what governs bank practice [FCAFC][20-149MR][20-166MR].
- **Lenders' own descriptions**: Westpac's living expenses page says HEM "provides lenders with a rough estimate of what a household like yours, in your location, would spend in a month depending on your lifestyle"; Macquarie's broker guide explains HEM is built into its serviceability calculator and declared expenses are compared with it [W][M].

## What this means for a borrower (the questions people ask)

- Declaring less than HEM does not lift borrowing power: the lender uses HEM. Declaring more reduces it: the lender uses your figure. The HEM is a floor, not a target.
- Rent or a mortgage is not in HEM; neither are school fees, childcare, insurance premiums, HECS or loan repayments. Those are added on top and are where most of the variation between applicants sits.
- HEM differs by lender only in the margin and income scaling each applies; the underlying index is the same.
- Banks verify expenses from three to six months of statements for the committed items and plausibility-check the rest against HEM; the 2019 case did not change that for the major lenders because APRA's guide already required declared expenses to be used where higher.
