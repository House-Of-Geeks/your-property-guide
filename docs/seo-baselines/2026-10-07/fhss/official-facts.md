# First Home Super Saver scheme: official rules, checked 7 October 2026

Sources are the ATO's FHSS pages (republished 8 July 2026), Guidance Note GN 2024/1, the Taxation Administration Act 1953 Sch 1 and ITAA 1997 Div 313, legislation.gov.au, the budget papers, and the Australian Government FHSS fact sheet. Figures are copied exactly and wording is paraphrased. Codes point to the source list at the end. "ATO-FHSS" stands for `https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/super/withdrawing-and-using-your-super/early-access-to-super/first-home-super-saver-scheme`.

## What changed on 15 September 2024 (our guide predates this)

The Treasury Laws Amendment (2023 Measures No. 3) Act 2023, Schedule 4, commenced on 15 September 2024 [L14].

- **Determination deadline:** you request it before ownership transfers to you (generally settlement), not before you sign a contract [A1][A5][L1].
- **Release after signing:** you can request the release up to **90 days after signing** the contract, if your determination was made on or after 15 September 2024. It was 14 days before then [A7][L10].
- **Notifying the ATO:** within **90 days** of signing (previously 28) [A8][L11].
- **Changing your mind:** you can amend or revoke a determination, and withdraw or amend a release request [L2][L6][L7][A12].
- **Transitional relief:** people who tried unsuccessfully between 1 July 2018 and 14 September 2024 and now own property can apply until 14 September 2027 [L7][A12].

## Limits and contributions

- **Limits:** $15,000 of contributions counted per financial year and **$50,000 in total**, from 1 July 2017. The total was $30,000 until it was raised for determinations requested from 1 July 2022 [A5][L4].
- **Eligible contributions:**
  - Voluntary concessional (before-tax) contributions: salary sacrifice, and personal contributions you claim a tax deduction for.
  - Voluntary non-concessional (after-tax) contributions [A4].
- **Not eligible:** [A4]
  - super guarantee
  - contributions from a spouse, parent or anyone else
  - award or fund-rule contributions
  - government co-contributions
  - downsizer contributions
  - anything over the contribution caps
- **The 85% / 100% rule:** you can release 100% of non-concessional contributions and 85% of concessional ones (for example, $25,000 salary sacrificed in one year counts as $15,000, of which $12,750 is releasable) [A5].
- **Order of counting:** first in, first out, by the date the fund received the contribution. Non-concessional contributions count first when made at the same time [L3][A5].
- **Under 18:** contributions made before you turned 18 can be included [A3].

## Associated earnings

- **Deemed, not actual:** your fund's real earnings don't matter. Earnings are calculated at the shortfall interest charge (SIC) rate, compounding daily, from the first day of the month of each contribution to the day the determination is made [A5][L5].
- **How the SIC rate is set:** the 90-day bank bill rate plus 3 percentage points, updated quarterly [A14].
  - Oct–Dec 2026: **7.51%** a year
  - Jul–Sep 2026: 7.43%
  - Apr–Jun 2026: 6.96%

## Tax

- **Going in:** concessional contributions are taxed at 15% in the fund [A1].
- **On release:** the assessable amount (85% of the concessional contributions plus all associated earnings) is taxed at your marginal rate plus Medicare levy, **less a 30% non-refundable offset**, in the year you request the release. Non-concessional amounts aren't taxed again [A18][L8][L9][A10].
- **Withholding:** the ATO withholds your estimated marginal rate including Medicare levy, less 30%, or 17% if it can't estimate it. You receive an FHSS payment summary [A9].
- **If you don't buy, recontribute or notify:** 20% FHSS tax on the assessable amount, on top of income tax [A13][L13].
- **Other income tests:** the released amount is excluded from family assistance, Medicare levy surcharge, study-loan repayment thresholds and child support. Salary-sacrificed contributions do count in study-loan repayment income in the year they're made [A18][A2][A4].
- **Division 293:** the released amount is subtracted from Division 293 income [A16].

## Eligibility

- **Age:** 18 or over to request a determination [A3][L1].
- **Never owned property in Australia:** this covers investment property, vacant land, commercial property, certain leases of land and company title. Inherited property counts once it's transferred to you [A3][A18].
  - **Exception:** if you lost all your property interests through financial hardship (bankruptcy, relationship breakdown, job loss, serious illness, natural disaster), you can apply for a hardship determination first [A11].
- **Individuals, not households:** couples, siblings or friends can each use their own FHSS money for the same property. Your name must be on the title [A3].
- **No completed previous release.** A withdrawn request or revoked release authority doesn't count [A3][L1].
- **Citizenship or residency:** not required [A1][A18].
- **Living in it:** move in as soon as practicable and live there for at least 6 of the first 12 months. The price must be at least the amount released [A2][L10].
- **What you can buy:** [A2][A5][A18]
  - A home, including off the plan.
  - Building a home (land plus a construction contract).
  - Not vacant land alone, houseboats, motor homes, or an investment property.

## Process (myGov: ATO online services > Super > Manage > First home saver) [A6]

1. **Request a determination** before ownership transfers. You can request more than one, and amend one. A release request should follow within 60 days unless the ATO allows more time [A6][L6].
2. **Request the release** [A7].
   - Only one release is allowed.
   - It can be changed or cancelled until the ATO starts processing it.
   - It takes **15 to 20 business days**; 95% were done within 20 business days in 2025–26 [A7][A9][A17].
3. **Sign a contract or recontribute** between 90 days before the release request and 12 months after it. The ATO may extend this by up to 12 months, to 24 in total, generally without an application [A8][L10].
4. **Notify the ATO** within 90 days of signing [A8].
5. **If you don't buy:** either recontribute the assessable amount less the tax withheld, as non-concessional contributions (you can't use FHSS again), or keep it and pay the 20% FHSS tax [A8][L12][A13].

## Combining with other schemes

- **Other schemes:** the Australian Government FHSS fact sheet (Oct 2025) says you can combine FHSS with other federal or state home buying schemes [F2]. Help to Buy's exclusions name guarantees, shared equity and state loans, not FHSS [F3].
- **State grants and duty concessions:** these are separate. Check with the state whether using FHSS affects them [A2].

## No changes announced

- No FHSS measure in the 2025–26 Budget, MYEFO 2025–26 or the 2026–27 Budget [B1][B2][B3][B4].
- Act No 86 of 2026 (from 1 Oct 2026) reworded "real property" in the eligibility section. There is no ATO guidance on any practical effect [L15].
- Treasury's own FHSS estimator still uses the old $30,000 limit and a 4.78% rate [T2].

## Usage (ATO, as at 2 July 2026) [A17]

| Year | Determinations | Release requests | Requested | Payments | Paid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025–26 | 54,700 | 18,400 | $386.7m | 16,100 | $330.3m |
| 2024–25 | 52,300 | 18,300 | $364.4m | 15,200 | $303.6m |

- **Average size:** about $21,000 requested per release in 2025–26 (our division; the ATO publishes no average).
- **Who used it:** 66% of release requests came from people earning $45,001–$135,000, and 28% from people aged 26–30.
