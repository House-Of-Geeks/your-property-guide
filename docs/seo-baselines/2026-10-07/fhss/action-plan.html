First Home Super Saver action plan

About 22,000 searches a month name the First Home Super Saver scheme, and another 11,000 ask whether super can be used to buy a house. They want the limits, whether it beats saving in the bank, how it’s taxed and how long the money takes. Our guide answers none of that with numbers, quotes a timing rule that changed in 2024, and barely shows on Google or in Copilot. This plan rewrites it, adds the only up-to-date FHSS calculator, and answers the plain-English question directly.

2

Build the FHSS calculator

New toolWeeks 1–3 · medium

The most-asked question is whether FHSS beats saving in the bank, and the answer depends on your income. Treasury’s estimator still runs on 2017 settings. Ours would be the current one.

  • Inputs: taxable income, how you contribute (salary sacrifice or after-tax), amount a year, number of years, a bank interest rate to compare.
  • Outputs: what counts toward the caps, the releasable amount (85% and 100% rules), deemed earnings at the SIC rate, tax withheld (marginal plus Medicare, less 30%), the deposit in your hand, and the same money saved after tax in a bank.
  • Keep the rules (caps, SIC rates by quarter, tax rates) in one dated data file, tested, so the guide and the calculator agree. The 2026–27 income tax rates and Medicare levy must be confirmed from the ATO before it ships.

The direct answer: generally no, except through FHSS, which lets you withdraw voluntary contributions (not super guarantee) for a first home. The page explains what you can and can’t do, then points to the guide and the calculator. Check any statement about SMSFs or early release grounds against the ATO before publishing.

4

Cover the process and the edge cases

SectionsWeeks 2–3 · small
  • Putting money in: salary sacrifice versus personal contributions you claim a deduction for (the notice of intent to your fund), and how that answers “how would they know it was me who contributed?”
  • Couples, siblings, friends: each person has their own $50,000 for the same home.
  • What you can buy: a home, including off the plan or land with a building contract; not vacant land alone or an investment property.
  • If you don’t buy: recontribute or pay 20% FHSS tax; extensions to 24 months.
  • Tax side-effects: HECS (salary sacrifice counts in repayment income; the release doesn’t), Division 293, the tax return.
  • With other schemes: FHSS can be combined with the 5% Deposit Scheme, Help to Buy, grants and duty concessions.
