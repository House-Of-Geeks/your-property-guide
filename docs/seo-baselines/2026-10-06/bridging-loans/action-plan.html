Bridging loans action plan

People searching for bridging loans are mostly homeowners trying to buy their next home before the current one sells. They already know what the product is. They want the cost in dollars, the risks, the cheaper options and which banks offer one. This plan sets out what to fix, build and write, in order, so our pages answer those questions while the spring selling peak is on.

2Actions in detail

1

Correct the cost figures in the guide

FixWeek 1 · small
Why

Cost is the most-asked question about bridging loans. The guide's "True cost of bridging" section and the FAQ "How much extra does bridging cost vs selling first?" say a $500,000 bridge over 6 months adds "roughly $2,500" of interest. That counts only the 1-point rate margin. If you sell first you never borrow that $500,000. With a bridging loan you pay the full rate on it.

What to change in /guides/bridging-loans-guide
  • Cost paragraph and FAQ. Interest on the bridged amount at the full rate: about $23,700 for $500k over 6 months at 9.3%, capitalised monthly. With the guide's own fee and holding-cost range, the total is about $25,000 to $27,000 before any rent saved.
  • Worked example. It shows "Capitalised bridging interest (6 months @ 7.5%): ~$28,000". On the $1.05m that the sale repays it is about $49,800 at 9.3% (about $40,000 even at the guide's own 7.5%). Peak debt becomes about $2,035,000 (78% LVR, still under the 80% cap) and end debt about $985,000.
  • Add a cost table. Use the amounts people search for, at an example rate inside the published bank rates (9.3%), with interest capitalised monthly; label it as an example and date it.

Lead every FAQ with a direct answer

FixWeek 1 · small
Why

An AI Overview appears on all 16 results pages. The answers it cites are short and specific. The guide's FAQ uses our own wording, not the questions people ask.

Rewrite the FAQ around these questions, each answered in one or two sentences
  • How much does a $100,000 bridging loan cost? 11 of 16 SERPs
  • What are the downsides of a bridging loan? 11 of 16
  • Is there a cheaper alternative to a bridging loan? 13 of 16, answer briefly and link to action 6
  • How much equity do you need for a bridging loan? 6 of 16
  • Can you repay a bridging loan early? 5 of 16
  • How long can a bridging loan last? 4 of 16
  • Do you make repayments during a bridging loan? Bing, four phrasings
  • What is the difference between open and closed bridging? 180 searches a month; the guide never uses either term

Keep the FAQ structured data in step with the visible questions. Update the page's updatedAt.

Done when each question above appears word for word as an FAQ heading, with the answer in its first sentence.

Add risk and scenario sections

SectionWeeks 3–4 · small to medium
Why

Bing already ranks the guide at positions 1–7 for long, specific questions that no competing page answers. These are some of them, verbatim:

Sections to add
  • What if your home doesn't sell? What happens at the end of a 6- or 12-month term, what happens if the price falls, the lender's options (extension, refinance, forced sale) and how to protect yourself (a sell-by date, a realistic price, an auction). Link to the suburb's market data.
  • Keeping the old home as an investment. Splitting the loan into a home loan and an investment loan. Add a note that interest deductibility follows what the money was used for, and point readers to an accountant.
  • Renting out the old home while it is for sale.
  • Downsizers and retirees. How the end-debt test works with little or no income. Link to and from /guides/downsizers-guide.
  • Building while you live in the old home. Construction bridging, about 130 searches a month.
  • Stamp duty timing when buying before selling.
Done when each Bing question above has a heading that answers it.

3What we leave alone

  • Commercial, development, fast and private bridging. About 450 searches a month, with bids of A$55–67 from specialist lenders. These searchers are not our sellers.
  • Recommending a lender or a loan. We give general, dated, sourced information only.
  • Going after "what is a bridging loan" directly. The banks and Bridgit hold it. The guide already covers it; we compete on cost, risk and the seller's situation first.

5The evidence

Why people want a bridging loan, strongest evidence first:

  • To buy the next home before the current one sells. Every bank product works this way, and every long Bing question is about it.
  • To secure a purchase while the deposit is tied up (deposit bonds, about 2,300 a month).
  • To downsize, often retirees on little income.
  • To build while living in the old home.
  • To buy at auction or settle fast.
  • To keep the old home as a rental instead of selling it.
