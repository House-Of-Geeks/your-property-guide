Help to Buy action plan

About 27,000 Australians a month search for Help to Buy and shared equity. They want the figures, what sharing ownership with the government means for them, what they can afford, and the rules for living with it. Google has crawled our guide and chosen not to index it, because it gives none of those figures. Copilot, meanwhile, cites it more than almost any page on the site, including three statements that are now wrong. This plan rewrites it with the numbers, adds a calculator, and covers the questions nobody answers in plain terms.

Why the guide isn’t indexed. It gives no figures: no income limits, price caps, lenders or dates, and it tells readers to treat its points “as the shape of the rules rather than fixed numbers”. It also has three statements that are now wrong (action 1). Our own July news article has the current income limits; the guide doesn’t. We appear on none of the 16 Google results pages.
Copilot already relies on it. Bing’s AI Performance report shows 3,400 Copilot citations of the guide between 6 July and 4 October. They come from 12 grounding queries: “help to buy scheme” (1,800, a 12.3% citation share), “help to buy scheme 2026” (665), “help to buy” (300), “help to buy scheme australia” (216), “shared equity scheme” (129) and others. Copilot is passing on what the page says, so the corrections in action 1 matter now, not just for Google. Keep the sections it cites and the “2026” in the title.

4

Build the Help to Buy calculator

New toolWeeks 2–3 · medium
Why

“help to buy calculator” grew from 20 to 260 searches a month in a year. People Also Ask is full of affordability questions: the deposit for a $700,000 house (7 of 16), the income for a $700,000 mortgage (6), the income for a $500,000 house (6). None of the ranking pages works out the money for you.

Build at /help-to-buy-calculator
  • Inputs: state and capital or regional area, new or existing, price, deposit, income, single, couple or single parent.
  • Eligibility check: income against the limit and price against the cap, with the reason if either fails.
  • Outputs: the government’s share (up to 30% or 40%), your loan, monthly repayments, the same purchase under the 5% Deposit Scheme, and what the government receives at sale after an assumed growth rate.
  • Keep the rules in one data file, with each figure’s source and date, so the guide and the calculator print the same numbers and the 1 July update is one edit.
Done when it opens on a worked example, matches the guide’s example, and the limits and caps are tested.
5

Cover living with the scheme

SectionWeeks 2–3 · small to medium

Autocomplete has about 32 questions here, and none of the ranking pages answer them plainly. One short, question-led section each:

  • Buying back the share: at least 5% of today’s value each time (or all of it), at a valuation you pay for.
  • If your income rises: reviewed at least every 5 years. Only 2 years in a row over the limit triggers anything. You repay at least 5% within 90 days if the lender finds you can afford it, and never if it would push you into LMI.
  • Selling: the government gets its percentage of the sale price or a valuation, whichever is higher; the bank is repaid first.
  • Renovating: notify before $21,000 or more in 12 months, or any work needing council approval, so the added value stays yours.
  • Renting it out: not allowed, except for Defence postings, employer relocations and serious illness.
  • Refinancing, separation and death: what Housing Australia allows in each case.
  • New, existing, off the plan or building: the 40% and 30% split, and moving in within 3 months of completion.

Decide the lead path

DecisionBefore action 4 ships

Help to Buy readers are buyers, mostly first home buyers on lower and middle incomes, not sellers, so the appraisal step that fits bridging doesn’t fit here. The options:

  • the buyer match (/find-an-expert), which sells buyer leads
  • the first home buyer guide funnel
  • a referral to a participating lender or broker

This is a commercial call for you.

3What we leave alone

  • The UK and Irish Help to Buy schemes. They fill autocomplete (Martin Lewis, ISA, Dublin, Kerry), and none of it is our audience.
  • The adjacent schemes, for now. First Home Super Saver (about 21,000 a month), first home owner grants (18,000), the 5% Deposit Scheme (19,700) and “first home buyer schemes” (24,000) are a bigger opportunity in their own right. They need their own plan.
  • Lender rates and recommendations. List the participating lenders with a date; don’t compare or rank them.
